# Shame Post Template – LinkedIn Version
TrollProof Playbook – Public Shame + Deterrence Loop

**Purpose:** Professional, longer-form LinkedIn post (or article) to expose a weak patent demand letter from a troll/NPE.
Goal: Build credibility in your network, attract sympathy/support from founders/investors, pressure troll to drop quietly (LinkedIn visibility often kills these faster than X), and deter future shakedowns.

**Important notes (delete before posting):**
- **Anonymize heavily** — redact names, firm, full patent number, company identifiers. Use "a small AI startup" or "a bootstrapped edtech tool".
- Post from your personal/professional profile (not company page if early-stage).
- Use article format if >1,300 characters (better reach).
- Attach redacted letter screenshot (blur everything identifying except claim language).
- Tag relevant people/groups sparingly: #PatentTroll #Startups #Innovation #AI #EdTech
- If troll escalates → consider unpublishing or adding update ("matter resolved").
- This is **not** legal advice — if suit threatened, consult attorney first.

**Post Template (copy-paste & customize):**

[Start with hook image: redacted letter screenshot or simple graphic saying "Another Day, Another Demand Letter"]

**Title (for article/post):**
Received a Patent Demand Letter as a Bootstrapped Founder — Here's What Happened (Anonymized)

**Body:**

Like many bootstrapped founders, I recently received a patent infringement demand letter from a non-practicing entity (NPE).

The claim: my small [niche description, e.g. "AI-powered tool for personalized learning"] allegedly infringes their broad patent on "[paraphrased vague claim, e.g. 'using artificial intelligence to customize educational content']".

Key observations (redacted letter attached):
- No detailed claim chart provided — just a general assertion.
- Claims appear highly abstract → strong §101 Alice ineligibility argument (mental process implemented on generic computing/AI).
- No evidence of proper marking under 35 U.S.C. §287 — which limits potential damages significantly.
- Owned by [redacted – "a holding entity with no products"] — classic NPE pattern.

I'm a solo founder building something useful for [brief value prop, e.g. "homeschool families supporting neurodivergent learners"]. No big funding rounds. No desire to spend time/money fighting in court.

But I also won't be extorted by low-effort shakedowns that target small builders.

So I'm sharing this publicly (anonymized) for a few reasons:
1. Transparency: Other founders should know this happens — often to very early-stage companies.
2. Deterrence: Public exposure frequently causes these letters to disappear quietly.
3. Community: If you've received similar letters from [vague description, e.g. "entities asserting broad AI personalization patents"], feel free to DM or comment (anonymously if preferred). Let's crowdsource prior art and patterns.

I responded firmly requesting a real claim chart, §287 proof, and §101 analysis. No response yet.

To my fellow founders: keep shipping. Don't let noise slow you down.

To the sender: if this is a good-faith claim, provide the evidence. If not — reconsider.

Grateful for this community.
