# DIY Patent Scan Checklist
TrollProof Playbook – Infringement Shield

**Goal:** Quickly spot obvious patent risks in your niche **without** hiring lawyers or using paid tools.
Do this quarterly (or before launching major features). Takes 30–60 minutes.
Not a full Freedom-to-Operate (FTO) opinion — just a founder-level red-flag check to pivot early.

**Important notes:**
- This is **DIY screening**, not legal advice.
- Focus on broad, obvious claims that could cover your core idea.
- If red flags appear → design around or consult attorney reactively.
- Document your scans (screenshots + date) for prior user rights / independent invention proof.

**Tools Needed (all free):**
- Google Patents: patents.google.com
- USPTO Patent Public Search: ppubs.uspto.gov
- Lens.org (good for non-US + academic prior art)
- Espacenet (worldwide patents)

   - List 3–5 key technical elements (avoid abstract ideas like "using AI to personalize learning").

[ ] 2. Brainstorm Search Keywords (5 min)
   - Core terms + synonyms
   - Combine with your niche: "AI" OR "machine learning" OR "generative" + "homeschool" OR "neurodivergent" OR "autism" OR "adaptive learning" OR "personalized education"
   - Add method words: "generate" OR "customize" OR "adapt" OR "prompt" OR "profile"
   - Negative terms (exclude): -patent -filing -application (to filter noise)

[ ] 3. Run Broad Search (10 min)
   - Go to Google Patents → search your top 3–5 keyword combos
   - Filter: Publication date last 20 years (most relevant)
   - Sort by: Relevance or Date (newest first)
   - Skim top 10–20 results:
     - Read **abstract** + **claims** (not description)
     - Look for independent claims (broadest)
   - Note any patents that sound scarily similar → copy title, number, assignee, date

[ ] 4. Dive Deeper on Hits (10–20 min)
   - For each worrying patent:
     - Read full independent claims (1, 8, 15 usually broadest)
     - Ask: Does my product literally do **every element** in a single claim?
       - If missing even one → likely no literal infringement
     - Check equivalents (Doctrine of Equivalents): Could a court say your version is "substantially the same"?
   - Check status: Active? Expired? (20-year term from filing date)
   - Note assignee: Big company? NPE/troll? Individual?

[ ] 5. Check Prior Art Exceptions (5 min)
   - Your own public disclosures (blog, X posts, demos) within 1-year grace period → protected in US (35 U.S.C. §102(b)(1))
   - Foreign risk: Most countries have **absolute novelty** — your own posts could kill foreign patents

[ ] 6. Red Flags & Action Items
   - [ ] Found patents with broad claims matching core elements → Plan design-around (change one key step/element)
   - [ ] High-risk troll indicators: NPE assignee, vague/abstract claims, recent assertions
   - [ ] No major hits → Good — but scan again in 3 months (new filings happen fast)

   - Timestamp: Date + "DIY scan – no professional advice"
   - Store privately (encrypted drive / secure note app)

**Quick Tips for Future-Proofing**
- AI patents exploding 2025+ — watch for "prompt engineering" + niche combos
- Abstract ideas (Alice §101) still kill many broad AI claims — note if hits seem vulnerable
- If international expansion planned → scan EPO/Espacenet early (no US grace period)
- Re-run before major launches, funding rounds, or partnerships
