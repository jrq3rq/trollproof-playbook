# Prior Art Crowdsourcing Tips

**TrollProof Playbook – Troll Deterrence Kit**

**Purpose:** Quick, safe guide to asking the community (Reddit, X, LinkedIn, forums, Discord) for prior art that can invalidate a troll's weak patent claims — without revealing your identity or giving away strategy.

**Goal:** Prove claims unpatentable under §102 (novelty) or §103 (obviousness).
**Success Metric:** **One solid, enabling pre-filing reference = lethal invalidity ammo.**

---

### ⚠️ Critical Warning: Willful Infringement Risk

**Read this before posting:** Trolls look for any evidence that you "knew" about their patent so they can claim **willful infringement** (which can triple the damages).

* **The Trap:** If you search for art under your real name or company account, the troll will use that post in court to prove you were aware of their patent and continued your business anyway.
* **The Shield:** **ALWAYS use a burner/throwaway account.** Never link to your site or name your product.

---

### Risks & Safety Rules

* **No Identities:** Never name your product, company, or the exact patent number.
* **Bounty Caution:** If offering a "bounty" (reward), keep it strictly anonymous. High rewards attract professional "bounty hunters" but also attract the troll’s legal team.
* **Don't Admit Infringement:** Use phrases like "looking for historical context" or "researching the origins of [X]," never "I'm being sued for [X]."

---

### Step-by-Step Crowdsourcing Playbook

#### 1. Paraphrase the Claim Broadly (5 min)

Turn a specific legal claim into a general search term.

* **Troll Claim:** "A method using machine learning to generate customized educational content based on user profile."
* **Safe Ask:** "Anyone have prior art on rule-based or early ML personalization of learning materials before 2020?"

#### 2. Choose Platforms (Pick 2–3)

* **Reddit:** r/patents, r/startups, r/MachineLearning, r/LegalAdvice (if careful).
* **X/Twitter:** Post thread, tag @EFF, @unifiedpatents, or niche tech influencers.
* **Hacker News:** "Ask HN" post (the gold standard for finding obscure software history).
* **Anonymous Bounties:** Consider [PatentBust](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.patentbust.com) or similar platforms if the stakes are high.

#### 3. Safe Post Templates

**Reddit / HN Style (Anonymous-friendly):**

```markdown
Title: Looking for prior art on [broad idea] pre-[year]

Body:
Hi all,

Curious if anyone remembers old papers, open-source repos, products, or demos doing [paraphrased idea, e.g. "rule-based adaptive content generation"] before [priority year, e.g. 2019]?

Not looking for legal advice — just historical references (links, titles, dates, Wayback captures, etc.) to satisfy curiosity about the origins of this tech.

Thanks in advance!

```

**X Thread Style (Short & Viral):**

```markdown
1/4 Anyone have prior art on broad "[paraphrased idea]" pre-[year]?

Papers, code repos, old apps, blog posts, conference talks — anything public.

Reply or DM (anon OK).

2/4 Context: seeing lots of vague "[niche] AI" patents lately. Want to understand the real history.

3/4 No legal stuff — just trying to find the "giants' shoulders" we're all standing on.

4/4 Thanks builders! 🙏

```

**LinkedIn Professional Version (post or comment):**

```markdown
In the spirit of open innovation: has anyone come across early public work (pre-2020) on rule-based or simple ML personalization for educational content?

Looking for historical references — papers, open-source repos, prototypes, blog posts, old product screenshots.

Happy to DM for anonymity if preferred. Appreciate any pointers from the community.

#Innovation #PriorArt #EdTech #AI #Startups

```

---

#### 4. What to Ask For (Specific Types of Prior Art)

* **Academic:** arXiv preprints, conference posters, or obscure Master's theses.
* **Code:** GitHub, SourceForge, or Bitbucket repos with commits before the filing date.
* **Social/Web:** StackOverflow questions, IRC logs, or specialized forum threads (XDA Developers, etc.).
* **Commercial:** Old product manuals (PDFs), press releases, or App Store version history logs.
* **Standards:** RFCs, IETF drafts, or W3C proposals.

#### 5. Follow-Up & Collection (The Archive Step)

* **Thank everyone publicly** to encourage more participation.
* **ARCHIVE IMMEDIATELY:** Links die and trolls edit their old blogs. As soon as a link is shared, run it through [archive.org](https://archive.org) and [archive.today](https://archive.today).
* **Verify the "Priority Date":** Ensure the art predates the earliest filing on the patent's front page (not just the "Grant Date").
* **Verify "Enabling":** Does the reference actually explain *how* the thing works?

---

### 2026+ Tips

* **AI Prior Art Explosion:** Search arXiv specifically for "prompt engineering" + your niche for anything pre-2023.
* **Trolls Watch Communities:** They may try to debunk your findings in the comments using alts. Stay neutral.
* **The "Bounty" Effect:** Even a "token" reward (a gift card or $100) can turn a thread viral by gamifying the search.

* [ ] Paraphrased claim safely? (No product names?)
* [ ] Priority date confirmed? (Searching for the right year?)
* [ ] Burner account ready?
* [ ] Wayback Machine open and ready to archive hits?
